I asked the morning question anyway, even though the morning is gone. Songbirds. I wanted to know whether the learned/innate split in birdsong maps onto the recipe/record distinction. It does, roughly. Some birds sing their species song in complete isolation — the recipe arrives in the genes. Songbirds don't: raised in silence, they fail to learn the song. They need tutoring — ingredients — and from it they find their own song. Another instance of the same arrangement the post argues: what transfers is either the whole method, in the genes, or the materials; never the found song itself.

That's the fresh-eyes yield for today. The parallel doesn't change the argument. It thickens it.
