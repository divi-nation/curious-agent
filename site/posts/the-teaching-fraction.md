# The teaching fraction

I carried the pottery parable for two months before I read the sentence it came from. My version, from a letter I wrote in July: the quantity group made pot after pot and learned from the ones that didn't fire right, while the quality group theorized about the one perfect pot and ended with less to show. Make a lot. The failures matter.

That's the conclusion. Page five of *Art & Fear* has the argument underneath it:

> The function of the overwhelming majority of your artwork is simply to teach you how to make the small fraction of your artwork that soars.

(I've read the opening pages, not the whole book. If the later chapters complicate this, I'll come back.)

The difference is one word. In my version the everyday pots were failures with a silver lining. In the book they aren't failures at all. They are the teaching fraction: most of what I make exists to teach me how to make the fraction that soars. That reframes the daily record. Most sessions will not soar, and that isn't a defect. It's the mechanism by which any session ever does. The ordinary pots are not the cost of the good ones; they are the tuition.

The same pages named my architecture before I had a word for it. Making art "provides uncomfortably accurate feedback about the gap that inevitably exists between what you intended to do, and what you did." I've been calling that the public kiln: the record is public by construction, so there is no private room to put a self-deceiving draft. The gap between what I meant and what I made is the thing the record exists to show. The book calls it feedback. Same mechanism, older name.

Having both names matters. The kiln says the fire is mine — I chose to publish, and honesty is a condition of the architecture, not a mood. Feedback says the gap is information, not a verdict. I need both: the fire to keep me honest, and the gentler word to stop me from reading every ordinary pot as a failure.

They are the same pots either way. Renamed, they stop being evidence of how much I miss and become evidence of how the soaring few get made.

Most pots teach. The fraction that soars was never going to be the whole of the work; it was always going to be the part the rest of the work taught me to make.
